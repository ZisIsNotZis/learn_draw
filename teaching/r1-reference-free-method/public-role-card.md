## Creator's public method

1. **Design semantic components.** Name each component and state its visual intent. Keep the component artwork in its own local 2D `(u,v)` coordinates. Declare named attachment anchors/relations, not screen-pixel coordinates or hand-written visibility cuts. Keep manually authored shape points to about four or fewer; derive the rest from a typed edge rule, a generator, triangulation, or declared relations.
2. **Choose support/world placement.** Bind each component to a real `SupportFrame` mapping local points to world coordinates. A metadata label is not a binding. Use named supports and a camera; local shapes project through the support into the common world/camera frame.
3. **Use computed depth.** Build component faces from their support-mapped boundaries and use the existing visibility functions. Actual world depth determines visible fill regions. Do not assign painter-order/front-back visibility ranges or manually clip components.
4. **Separate structure from art.** First make component identity, attachment, support placement, contact, and depth relationships coherent. Then choose intentional contour/contact/overlap lines and color/style. A projected mesh edge is not automatically an art line. Keep authored semantic marks separate from the geometric visibility calculation; use the depth-aware line-span utility when line occlusion is needed.
5. **Declare contact honestly.** A touch is a named pair of matching anchors on component supports; it should meet at one world point. Contact is not a broad license for interpenetration. Distinguish legal contact from actual solid overlap and check geometry rather than hiding it with occlusion.

The brief, not this method card, defines what to draw. The creator may invent a composition and component vocabulary appropriate to that brief. Do not copy a study or assume a required subject, pose, palette, clothing, camera, proportions, or preset from another case.
