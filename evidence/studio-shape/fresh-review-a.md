# Independent image review: coast

Viewed draft-fullres.png for detail and draft.png for layout. The images are visible and depict a flat-colour lighthouse on a cliff beside the sea, with a pale sun in the upper left.

What is wrong here?

- The lighthouse reads clearly at thumbnail size, but the cliff is less clear: the rounded green-grey top rests above very large dark slabs that run out of the bottom edge. Their broad, smooth shapes resemble overlapping blocks more than a continuous rocky landform.
- The pale foreground sea and the cliff's green-grey top have similar subdued values. The water boundary rises behind the island and meets it with little contrast, making that part of the shoreline ambiguous.
- The dark oval near the left horizon is isolated and almost horizontal; it reads as a floating mark without a clear relationship to the other land or water shapes. The lower-left oval has the same ambiguity.
- The sea has only a few short, very pale horizontal marks. Its large empty areas and the broad sweeping foreground boundary make it read as stacked flat colour regions rather than visibly receding water.
- At full resolution, the cream lighthouse body has a narrow grey-beige side strip but the lantern, roof, and base remain very flat. The result is a readable emblem with only a weak sense of consistent volume.

The composition is balanced and uncluttered, and the lighthouse silhouette is distinct. These observations concern the visible drawing only; no source or implementation was inspected.
