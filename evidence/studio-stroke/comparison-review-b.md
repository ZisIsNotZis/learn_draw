# Image-only comparison review B

Question: what is wrong here?

## Cactus A, B and C

All three show a dark green, one-armed cactus in a terracotta pot on a pale cream square. The oval main body, pot and large empty margins look effectively unchanged. The silhouette reads clearly, although it is extremely spare: there are no spines, ribs, soil, pot rim or surface cues. The main body reads somewhat like an upright leaf or oval rather than a columnar cactus.

A has a straight diagonal arm meeting a tall, flat-topped upright. The rounded square top and the small protruding corner at its outside elbow make the arm feel assembled from distinct pieces. The junction against the main body has a small uneven shoulder.

B replaces the upright with a short rounded capsule and makes the elbow turn more softly. Its rounded tip is less blocky than A. However, the upright narrows noticeably where it joins the diagonal branch, creating a pinched neck; it can look like a separate bud sitting on the arm. The concave notch between the body and branch is still sharp.

C has a taller, thicker-looking upright with a rounded dome, and a longer diagonal rise leading into it. The outside elbow is continuous and smoother than A's, and it avoids B's conspicuous pinched neck. Its silhouette is the most continuous of the three. Remaining defects are the abrupt angular inner corner where the diagonal meets the upright and the deep, sharp notch where the branch leaves the main body. The arm is also quite thick relative to the narrow body; this creates a heavy, raised-fist shape rather than a delicate branching plant. That is a visible stylistic proportion, not necessarily an error for an icon.

## Wheel A and B

Both show a four-colored pinwheel with a pale yellow circular hub on a brown stick. A has four straight triangular vanes, arranged diagonally around the hub. B has four visibly curved, hooked vanes, with paired darker and lighter color areas.

A reads as a clean, flat pinwheel symbol, but its broad triangles look like four separate pennants converging on a disk. There is little sense of a folded surface or of material turning around the hub. The large triangular gaps between blades make the center feel sparse. The lower vanes sit alongside and partly over the top of the stick without much visible depth information.

B has a much stronger visible sweep: each vane curls toward the center and the four curls establish a consistent rotational pattern. The alternating light and dark areas also distinguish inner and outer portions of the curved surfaces. Its overall silhouette is much less like a set of simple triangles.

B's main unresolved defect is at the center. The vanes narrow to thin tails as they reach the hub, especially the left and upper vanes, so they can look like ribbons attached by narrow necks. The yellow vane has a very thin horizontal entry at the right of the hub before expanding into a broad hanging curve. The lower salmon vane's inner end runs tightly along the stick, producing a crowded brown/salmon sliver under the disk. These features make attachment and layering difficult to read. The two-tone regions suggest folds, but do not yet make the central folded construction unambiguous. The bottom of the stick remains conspicuously tapered and softly rounded in both versions.

## Overall

The subjects are readable in every image. Cactus C removes the most obvious segmented elbow of A and the capsule-like neck of B, while preserving a sharp inner bend. Wheel B adds strong curvature and a clearer rotational gesture, while introducing thin, crowded joins near the hub. The remaining weaknesses are primarily silhouette transitions and the readability of central attachments, rather than color balance or composition.
