# Raw image-only review B

Viewed only the full-resolution and thumbnail render for each drawing.

## Waving character

Recognizability is strong: a smiling, blue-haired person wearing a terracotta long-sleeved top and raising an open hand. The gesture remains legible at thumbnail size. Hair, face, ears, neck, garment and raised hand are distinguishable.

The most consequential weakness is the raised arm. Its shoulder-to-elbow-to-wrist route forms a uniformly bent tube with little anatomical change at the elbow. The dark inner stripe makes it read partly as a separate inset strip rather than a form turning through light. The arm also appears to emerge from behind the side of the torso with an abrupt meeting: the torso boundary continues almost straight past the upper-arm join. This is readable, but the attachment lacks a convincing shoulder/underarm transition.

The lowered arm on the viewer's left is a thin wedge running beside the oversized torso and leaving the frame. Its relationship to the shoulder is unclear; a small extra rounded sliver at the shoulder looks like a redundant contour. The composition's bottom crop prevents the viewer from seeing how that arm or the garment ends. A cropped portrait is a legitimate choice, but here the large blank sweater mass dominates the face and gesture.

The hand is recognizable and all five digits can be separated. It is disproportionately small and fine-lined relative to the large sleeve and head. The wrist narrows abruptly, with a slightly angular skin-to-cuff meeting. The fingers have little joint or knuckle modulation, which is an acceptable simplified graphic treatment, not a broken hand. Palm crease lines are very light and contribute little at the small size.

The face is readable and friendly. The eyes have much heavier outlines and more internal contrast than the nose, mouth or hand. Their dark lash mass and nearly horizontal shapes give a more fixed, half-lidded expression than the energetic wave suggests. Slight asymmetry is not intrinsically a defect. The central nose is a small angular mark and the mouth a simple curve, both reasonable stylistic simplifications.

Hair silhouette reads clearly as a bob. The lower back-hair edge is a very straight shelf behind the jaw, and the long pointed side locks terminate abruptly. This gives it a somewhat helmet-like construction. A thin doubled-looking edge near the crown and side outline is visually fussier than the otherwise flat style. The large bang's meeting with the forehead is legible.

Colour is cohesive and restrained. Flat skin and garment fills are appropriate to an illustration. The lone strong shaded strip on the raised arm is inconsistent with the otherwise mostly unshaded figure; it suggests a lighting/form treatment that the torso, hair and face do not follow. Dark outlines mostly hold together, but the very fine hand and hair interior lines lack the same visual weight as face and garment edges.

No catastrophic recognition or detached-part defect is visible. The arm construction, shoulder connection and ambiguous lowered arm are substantive drawing weaknesses; omitted fabric texture, flat colour, sparse facial marks and a cropped body are style choices.

## Cactus

Immediately recognizable as a minimal potted cactus, including at thumbnail size. The single raised branch connects continuously to the central body; there is no visible gap, seam or accidental notch. The pot masks the lower stem cleanly.

There is no serious contour or part-connection failure. The central body is a tall oval with a pointed-round tip, while the branch bends upward like a smooth hook. This is a plausible emblematic cactus silhouette, although the central stem is more leaf-like than columnar. With no ribs or spines, recognizability rests mainly on the branch and pot; it succeeds, but the form vocabulary is generic.

The pot reads as a solid tapered terracotta container. There is no rim ellipse, opening, soil or visible depth, so the cactus seems to enter a flat trapezoid rather than a spatial vessel. That is a legitimate flat-icon abstraction. It would be a limitation only if the intended result were a volumetric drawing or a more observational plant study.

The green, terracotta and warm cream work well together. No outline, texture, shadow or gradient is needed for this icon style. The smooth silhouette, rounded corners and broad negative space are coherent. The branch being on only one side is a design choice rather than a defect. Compared with the character, this image is more internally consistent, but it demonstrates simple silhouette design much more than articulated form.
