# Fresh image-only review

Viewed only draft-fullres.png and draft.png.

The watering action reads: the can tips toward the plant, the spout points toward the pot, and the continuous curved stream lands inside the soil opening. The can is no longer merely being carried beside a plant. I see no serious failure in the pouring destination or the overall spatial grouping.

The main remaining weakness is the hand/handle connection. The pale hand appears as a small horizontal fist resting over the arch, but the arch does not visibly pass through a grip. Its top is hidden by the hand and its left end resumes below and left of the fingers; the fingers have shallow interior lines without a decisive thumb-versus-fingers enclosure. At thumbnail size this can read as contact, but it is less convincing than the rest of the action. Strengthening one clear overlap where fingers wrap around the handle would help more than adding anatomical detail.

The hand and sleeve transition is also abrupt: the raised sleeve reaches behind a flat, nearly rectangular hand, with little readable wrist direction. That weakens the sense that the arm is supporting the tilted can. This is a local articulation issue, not a demand for realistic hands.

The character looks toward the viewer instead of the pot. This makes the pose feel presented rather than absorbed in watering; it does not invalidate the action. The extended arm, can and plant otherwise form a clear left-to-right action chain, and the window provides a quiet enclosing shape.

Graphic finish is uneven. The face and hair use dark contours and small internal details, whereas the torso, can, plant and window are largely unoutlined silhouettes. That makes the head feel more carefully finished than the action-bearing hand and can. Hair tips are especially thin and sharp compared with the broad, softened forms elsewhere. These are style-coherence weaknesses rather than structural failures.

The flattened can, simple leaves, single-line stream, oversized head and minimal clothing are legitimate flat-cartoon simplifications. No realistic perspective, splash, shading, leaf texture or extra fingers are needed for this scene to work. The narrow priority is a more legible grip, followed by a consistent level of graphic finish.
