# Raw image-only review

Viewed only draft-fullres.png and draft.png.

The subject and action read immediately: a blue-haired person is watering a potted plant beside a window. The can, spout, water and pot form a clear chain. The stream visibly reaches the soil inside the rim. I do not see a serious pouring-target or plant/pot attachment error.

The main weakness is the grip. The peach hand reads as a rounded rectangular cap sitting above the yellow handle, with a couple of short finger creases. It does not convincingly show fingers curling around the handle or a thumb opposing them. At thumbnail size the hand almost disappears into a small pale block. The handle does connect beneath it, so this is an under-explained grip rather than a visibly detached can. More explicit overlap between finger, handle and palm would make the weight-bearing relationship credible.

The raised arm is readable as a long sleeve, but its large unbroken salmon silhouette gives little evidence of elbow and wrist. Its upper contour sweeps into the back of the hand while its lower contour broadens into the can area. This makes the pose somewhat rubbery and schematic. The downward arm is also cut by the bottom edge without a hand. That crop is permissible, but together with the broad torso it leaves the person feeling like an unfinished bust attached to an action arm.

Composition is clear and uncluttered. The person anchors the left, the plant anchors the right, and the can bridges them. The window provides a coherent spatial backdrop. The substantial empty lower-right area makes the activity sit high in the frame, while the body is cropped below; this is a balance issue, not a broken scene. The plant pot is supported by the sill and reads as sitting there.

The water is a single smooth opaque teal stroke. This is an acceptable flat-illustration convention and its trajectory is understandable, although its colour is close to the pot and it can initially read as a thin hose or wire. A modest visual cue that it is flowing water would strengthen the action. The spout/body join and the tilted can are otherwise legible.

The strongest stylistic mismatch is between the carefully outlined, internally detailed face/hair and the largely unoutlined clothing, hand, can and plant. The face looks imported from a more detailed drawing language. The heavy dark hair contour and detailed eyes attract more attention than the watering interaction. This is a finish/cohesion issue rather than a spatial failure.

The warm cream, salmon, muted blue, mustard and green palette is pleasant and sufficiently separated. Flat fills, simplified leaves, lack of cast shadows and a nearly frontal window are ordinary choices for this style, not faults in themselves. The plant reads clearly despite its simplicity. The image needs the grip and arm articulation clarified more than it needs extra rendering or decorative detail.

Overall: the watering action succeeds. The hand/handle grip is the consequential remaining drawing weakness; the arm shape, crop balance and inconsistent line treatment are secondary. There is no obvious catastrophic occlusion or impossible pouring relationship in these images.
