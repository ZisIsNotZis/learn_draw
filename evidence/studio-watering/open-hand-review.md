# Raw image-only review

Viewed only `open-hand-check/draft-fullres.png` and `open-hand-check/draft.png`.

The scene reads immediately as a person watering a potted plant by a window. The plant, watering can, and curved stream form a clear action group, including at thumbnail size. The restrained warm/cool palette and uncluttered setting work well.

## Serious issue

The hand does not convincingly hold the watering can. It is an open, horizontally spread hand resting across the top of the handle, with all the long fingers extending toward the right. No finger visibly wraps around the handle or closes against the thumb. The handle disappears beneath the palm, but that overlap reads as a hand laid over an object rather than a load-bearing grasp. As a result, the can appears suspended below an open hand. This is the main failure of the action, despite watering being recognizable from the other objects.

## Secondary issues

- The wrist/hand joins the sleeve as a small rectangular cuff-like block. Combined with the splayed fingers and raised thumb, the gesture looks closer to presenting or releasing the can than controlling its tilt.
- The can is tilted, but the arm and open hand provide little sense of the torque or weight needed to keep it in that position. A simplified body pose is acceptable here; the missing gripping gesture is what makes the physical relationship weak.
- The face has much finer line work and internal detail than the body, can, and plant. This gives it a slightly different visual finish. The contrast is noticeable at full size and less important at thumbnail size.
- The extended arm and can overlap the window framing and sill densely. The silhouettes remain understandable, but the action would read more cleanly with slightly more breathing room around the hand/handle relationship.

## Acceptable flat-illustration conventions

The simple torso, absent garment folds, flat fills, minimal plant construction, and unshaded pot do not themselves prevent the scene from working. The large window provides a useful background frame. The cropped lower arm/body is a composition choice rather than an anatomical error. The curved water stream reaching the pot is readable as stylized water even without droplets or a splash.

Overall: clear subject and scene, pleasant composition and colour, but the open hand is a substantive interaction problem. The drawing communicates watering through the can, stream, and plant rather than through a believable hand holding the can.
