# Fresh image-only review

Viewed only draft-fullres.png and draft.png.

The scene reads immediately as a person watering a plant. The restrained palette, broad shapes, unshaded clothing, simplified leaves, and sparse facial features are a coherent flat illustration treatment; their lack of modelling is not itself a defect.

## Main remaining issues

1. **The can's attitude weakens the pouring action.** Its bottom rises toward the spout, so the reservoir appears tipped away from the outlet while a sustained stream leaves the raised spout. A sufficiently full can could do this, but that fullness is invisible. The readable gesture is holding the can up beside the plant more than actively tipping it to pour. The stream supplies most of the action.
2. **The grip is understandable but still diagrammatic.** The skin silhouette covers the crown of the handle and the two gold handle legs emerge below it, so there is a real grasp connection. However, the broad rectangular upper hand, long horizontal finger divisions, and tiny isolated crease at the right edge make the hand read as a mitten or block wrapped over a bar. The thumb-versus-fingers overlap is not strongly resolved. At thumbnail size it becomes a pale rectangle perched on the handle. This is a local hand-articulation weakness, not a missing hand-object connection.
3. **The water's destination is visually underexplained.** The arc reaches the upper edge of the pot, beside the stem, but the pot has no visible opening or soil plane. Consequently the stream can look as though it meets the back rim or disappears behind a solid vessel. A flat style can still communicate an opening through a small overlap or interior shape.

## Secondary issues

- The extended sleeve's outer edge flows well from the torso, but its swollen lower contour and narrow wrist create a soft tube with little elbow direction. The pose is readable and calm; the arm's mechanics are less specific than the face and hair.
- The full-resolution face has dark, relatively intricate eyelids, irises, brows, and hair outlines, whereas the hand, clothing, can, and plant use much quieter edges. This draws attention to the face rather than the hand-and-pour interaction. It is a hierarchy imbalance, not a requirement to outline every object.
- The window's heavy vertical divider sits behind the spout area, and the horizontal divider runs through the leaves. These overlaps are legible, but the large window grid competes a little with the smaller action. The composition otherwise has a clear left-to-right sequence from person to hand to can to plant.
- The hanging arm is cropped at the bottom edge. This is an acceptable bust/half-body crop, though the narrow remnant at bottom left feels slightly accidental compared with the deliberately composed window and plant.

## What is already working

The hand, handle, and can form one visible chain; the can is not floating independently. The watering stream is easy to follow and reaches the plant's container. The plant silhouette is clean. The warm clothing and can contrast gently with the cool hair, window, and pot. The full scene remains readable at the small preview size. There is no need to add gradients, realistic anatomy, or detailed surface texture to solve the remaining action and contact ambiguities.
