# Raw image-only review

The action reads immediately at both sizes: a person holds a tilted watering can and sends water into a plant pot on a window sill. I see no serious failure of support, contact, or pouring direction.

- The weakest action detail is the hand/handle contact. At full resolution the peach hand sits across the upper end of the yellow handle, but the fingers do not clearly wrap around it. The lower peach strip looks like a finger resting over the can shoulder. It is plausible as a simplified grip, though less definite than the clear arm-to-hand and stream-to-pot connections. At thumbnail size it becomes a pale blob over the handle. One readable overlap showing the handle passing through a curled grip would make the weight-bearing hold more convincing.
- The arm connects continuously to the hand, the can hangs below that contact, and the can is visibly tilted. The stream leaves the spout, bends down, and lands inside the pot opening to the left of the stem. These relationships work; the shallow stream arc is not, by itself, a structural error.
- The pot bottom meets the sill and the plant stem enters the soil. The pot is large enough to ground the plant. There is no floating support failure.
- Composition is more uneven than the action: the head, shoulder and can gather in the upper-left/middle, while a large quiet area sits below the window on the right. The hanging left arm runs out of the bottom edge while the torso ends just above it. That asymmetric crop feels slightly accidental. A deliberate waist/arm crop or tighter lower framing would give the image a clearer finish.
- The window grid and thick rounded frame strongly organize the scene, but the horizontal crossbar lies very close behind the hand and handle. This crowds the most important small contact area. A little separation there would improve the grasp read.
- The face and hair have fine dark contours and small details, whereas the sleeve, watering can, pot and leaves mostly have unoutlined flat silhouettes. This gives the face disproportionate visual emphasis and makes the rest look less resolved beside it. It is a consistency issue, not evidence that every object needs an outline.
- The muted warm/cool palette is coherent. Yellow can, blue-green pot, green leaves and terracotta clothing remain distinguishable. The water is slender and low-contrast at thumbnail size, but is still traceable between spout and pot. A modestly stronger water value could help without adding rendering effects.

Flat sleeve volume, a minimally articulated elbow, planar can body, repeated simple leaves, absence of cast shadows and a stylized face are acceptable flat-cartoon conventions here. They should not be promoted into serious anatomical or depth defects. The main improvement is a clearer grip, followed by a more intentional lower crop and more consistent detail emphasis.
