# Independent image review B — face and hand

Viewed only draft-fullres.png and draft.png from usability/check-source-preserved. Asked: what is wrong here?

## Actually visible

A front-facing stylized head with blue hair and a small smile sits beside an isolated raised open hand. Five digits and several palm creases are visible. Both supplied images display the same drawing; neither is blank.

## Concrete visual defects

- The head and hand are disconnected objects. The hand ends in a diagonal wrist cutoff, and the head has neither neck nor shoulders. They read as separate demonstration pieces rather than one person making a gesture.
- The lower hair ends in two very sharp points beside the jaw and a straight horizontal dark-blue edge behind the face. This produces an abrupt triangular/boxy cut at the bottom instead of a convincing fall of hair.
- There are narrow doubled contour marks along the crown and outer hair edges, especially at upper left and near the right temple. They resemble close parallel outlines rather than clearly separated locks.
- The hand's thumb-root transition is angular: below the thumb there is a short inward notch followed by another outward corner before the wrist. The silhouette has an awkward kink there.
- The palm is very broad relative to the narrow wrist, with the lower palm tapering to an almost straight diagonal wrist edge. The transition feels stiff and cut out.
- The face's eye shapes are strongly horizontal and heavily outlined while the nose and mouth are very light single curves. This gives the eyes much greater visual weight than the other features.

## Layout at thumbnail size

The face and five-finger gesture remain readable. The thin palm creases nearly disappear, leaving the hand as a pale silhouette. The open space between hand and head makes their separation especially apparent; the severe lower hair points remain prominent even when small. No claim here depends on an inferred implementation cause.
