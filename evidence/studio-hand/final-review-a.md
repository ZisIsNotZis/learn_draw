# Independent image review

Reviewed only `usability/check-source-preserved/draft-fullres.png` and `draft.png` using image viewing. Detail observations below use the full-resolution image.

## What is visibly present

Both images visibly contain a blue-haired, smiling face on the left and an open palm on the right, against a pale grey-green background. Neither image appears blank. The smaller image preserves the same overall reading, with fine facial and palm marks much less legible.

The face has two open brown eyes, eyebrow curves, a short nose mark, and a curved smile. The hair forms a rounded cap, a swept fringe and pointed side locks. The palm has five distinct digits, rounded fingertips and a few crease marks. Finger separation is readable.

## What is wrong here?

1. The head and hand read as separate cutout icons. The head ends at the chin and the hand ends at a diagonally closed wrist; there is no neck, shoulder, arm or other visible connection establishing a person raising their hand. This is a visible limitation if the drawing is intended to read as a continuous character portrait.
2. The hair behind the face terminates in an unusually rigid horizontal shelf on both sides near the lower cheek/jaw area. Long pointed side locks descend below that shelf. Their combination makes the back hair look like a flat panel with detached-looking spikes rather than a clearly articulated haircut.
3. Around the outer upper sides of the hair cap there are closely spaced, nearly parallel contour strokes. They read as doubled outlines, especially where the cap meets the side hair, rather than clearly separated strands or planes.
4. The eyes are very shallow horizontal slits compared with the large uninterrupted face area. The heavy upper eye contours dominate; the iris circles are visibly cut by the narrow openings. The expression remains readable as a mild smile, but the eye treatment looks stiff and flattened.
5. The hand is recognizably an open palm, but its broad uninterrupted skin area and sparse creases make it read as a flat symbol. The thumb-root contour changes direction abruptly near the lower left palm, while the wrist has little structural transition before its closed edge. I do not see an extra or missing digit.
